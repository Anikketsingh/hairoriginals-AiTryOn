Section 2Open points needing a decision

Six items. Each is a choice the draft currently answers one way, stated below so you can confirm or overrule it. Two items from the previous revision are now closed and noted at the end.

2.1  Minors' facial images Highest exposure

The app is set to 16+, above the storefront's 13, because a facial image processed to generate a likeness is special-category data under GDPR Article 9 and "biometric information" under several US state statutes.

16 does not remove the exposure — it narrows it. A 16- or 17-year-old is still a minor, and their biometric data attracts the same treatment. The concrete risk is the Illinois Biometric Information Privacy Act: statutory damages of USD 1,000 per negligent violation and USD 5,000 per reckless violation, assessed per person, with a private right of action. BIPA also requires a written release and a published written retention and destruction schedule before collection — obligations that go beyond a tick-box.

Separately, no age verification is performed. The requirement is stated in the terms, but users are never asked to confirm their age, so nothing prevents a 12-year-old from uploading a photo.

Current position: 16+ stated in the terms; explicit tick-box consent before each upload; no age check.

Decisions needed: (a) Confirm 16, or raise to 18 to keep minors' biometric data out entirely. (b) Should we add an age self-declaration at signup, so the requirement is affirmative rather than merely stated? (c) Is tick-box consent sufficient, or do we need a distinct written biometric release and a published destruction schedule for BIPA? (d) Should we geo-block Illinois rather than accept the exposure?

2.2  No automated deletion mechanism exists High

The policy promises deletion within 30 days of a request. There is no self-service delete button and no automated deletion job in the product — the promise can only be met by someone reading the privacy mailbox and acting on it by hand.

This now depends on a single named address (see Section 1). A commitment we cannot operationally honour is worse than no commitment, because it converts an ordinary backlog into a breach of our own published policy.

Decision needed: Either (a) confirm a named owner and a backup will monitor the mailbox and action requests from launch, or (b) instruct us to build a self-service deletion flow before launch.

2.3  Photos are stored indefinitely, not discarded High

Both the uploaded photograph and every generated image are retained so users can revisit past try-ons in a "My Looks" gallery. Deleting the source photo immediately after generation would remove that feature but would sharply cut the volume of biometric data held at rest — and would largely answer the BIPA destruction-schedule point in 2.1.

Current position: retained while the account is active; deleted within 30 days of a request, subject to legal retention obligations.

Decision needed: Accept indefinite retention, or set a hard automatic expiry — for example, delete source photos after 30, 90, or 180 days whether or not the user asks?

2.4  Photos are sent to Google for processing Medium

Generation is performed by Google's Gemini API. The user's photograph and the product image leave our infrastructure and are processed by Google, potentially outside the user's own country. We do not use uploaded photos to train any model, and the policy says so.

The storefront privacy policy names no third-party processors at all, so this disclosure exists only in the app policy.

Current position: Google named explicitly; cross-border transfers stated to rely on the provider's standard contractual clauses or equivalent safeguards.

Decision needed: Confirm whether a Data Processing Addendum with Google is executed, and whether you want the specific transfer mechanism named in the text rather than described generically.

2.5  Cookie consent is shown only in Europe Medium

The consent banner appears based on the visitor's browser timezone: EEA, UK, and Swiss visitors must opt in before any analytics or advertising script loads. Elsewhere those scripts load without a prompt. Timezone detection is approximate — a VPN or a traveller defeats it.

One strictly necessary cookie meters free try-on credits and is not optional; this is disclosed.

Decision needed: Accept the region-based approach, or require opt-in for all visitors worldwide? Universal opt-in is easier to defend and removes the geolocation-accuracy problem, at some cost to analytics coverage and ad measurement.

2.6  Cookie consent cannot be withdrawn in the app Medium

The consent decision is stored in the visitor's browser and the banner is never shown again. There is no cookie-settings screen and no way to change the answer once given. GDPR Article 7(3) requires that withdrawing consent be as easy as giving it, so an accept-once-forever design is the weak point here — not the banner itself.

The policy therefore tells users the honest workaround: clearing the site's browser data resets the choice. That is accurate but is not what Article 7(3) has in mind.

Decision needed: Should we build a cookie-settings control that lets users change their answer, and a matching link in the app footer? This is a small piece of work and it removes the Article 7(3) argument entirely.

This policy explains what the HairOriginals AI Try-On app collects, why, who it is shared with, and how to get it deleted. It adds to the HairOriginals privacy policy rather than replacing it: the storefront policy governs browsing and purchases, while this one covers the photo you upload here and everything the try-on does with it.

Who controls your data

KrisOriginals Pvt. Ltd (CIN U51909HR2018PTC074470), trading as HairOriginals, of Plot No. 220, Udyog Vihar, Phase IV, Sector 18, Gurugram, Haryana – 122015, India, is the controller of the personal data described here. Privacy requests go to aniket.kushwaha@hairoriginals.com.

Purchases on the HairOriginals storefront are governed by the storefront privacy policy. Where the two documents differ on the handling of photos you upload to this app, this policy applies.

Your photo, and why it needs special consent

The core feature requires a photo of your face. A facial image processed to generate a likeness is treated as biometric / special-category data under the EU/UK GDPR (Article 9) and under US state biometric laws including the Illinois Biometric Information Privacy Act. We process it only on your explicit consent, which you give by ticking the consent box before uploading. The box is unticked by default and is asked again for each new photo, so consent is never assumed from a previous try-on. You can withdraw consent at any time by requesting deletion.

Your uploaded photo is stored, not discarded after processing, so your past try-ons remain visible in "My Looks". Both the original photo and each generated image are retained until you request deletion or the retention period below elapses.

We do not use your photo to train any AI model, and we do not use it for facial recognition, identification, or matching you against any database.

What else we collect

  • Phone number — used to sign you in via one-time code, and as your account identifier.
  • Name and email — only if you provide them when requesting a stylist consultation.
  • Device and usage data — a hashed device fingerprint, a hashed IP address, and a device cookie. These exist to meter free try-on credits and prevent abuse; this is strictly necessary to run the service.
  • Try-on activity — which products you viewed, tried, and saved, plus any feedback or rating you submit.

Who we share it with

  • Google (Gemini API) — receives your uploaded photo and the product image in order to generate the try-on. Processing occurs on Google infrastructure, which may be outside your country.
  • Supabase — database, authentication, and image storage.
  • Digicuro CRM — if you request a stylist consultation, your contact details and links to your generated looks are sent so a stylist can follow up.
  • SMS providers — your phone number is sent to the provider that delivers your one-time code (Nimbus for Indian numbers, Twilio elsewhere).
  • Meta — if you accept analytics cookies, we send advertising measurement events. Email and phone are hashed before sending; your IP address is sent to Meta as part of this.

Transfers outside your region rely on the relevant provider's standard contractual clauses or equivalent safeguards.

We do not sell your data

We do not sell, rent, or trade your personal data, and we never disclose it to anyone in exchange for money or other valuable consideration. That covers your photos, your generated looks, your phone number, and your try-on history. We have no data-broker relationships and we do not license your data to anyone.

The companies named above are service providers working on our instructions. They may use your data only to deliver the specific service we have asked them for, and not for their own purposes. Your photo goes to Google to generate your try-on and for nothing else — not to train their models, and not to build a profile of you.

To be straightforward about the one exception: if you accept analytics cookies, we send advertising measurement events to Meta. No money changes hands, but some US state privacy laws still classify that as "sharing" for cross-context behavioural advertising, so we would rather name it than let you find out later. Decline cookies and it never happens. If you have already accepted and want to undo it, clearing this site's data in your browser resets the choice and we will ask you again.

Where we operate

We are established in India and the app is operated from there, but it is available to users elsewhere. Where you are in the EEA, the UK, or a US state with its own privacy or biometric statute, we apply the protections that law gives you — including the consent, access, and deletion rights set out below — regardless of where we are based.

Cookies and tracking

A device cookie is set to track your free credits — this is strictly necessary and is not optional. Analytics and advertising scripts (Meta Pixel, Vercel Analytics) load only after you accept where consent is required, and never before. Declining leaves the app fully functional.

How long we keep it

Photos, generated images, and account data are kept while your account is active. If you request deletion we remove them within 30 days, except where we are required to retain records by law.

To ask for deletion, email aniket.kushwaha@hairoriginals.com from the address or phone number on your account, or contact the Grievance Officer below. Deleting your photos does not affect any order you have placed on the storefront.

Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to access, correct, delete, port, or restrict processing of your data, to object to processing, and to withdraw consent at any time without affecting prior processing. EEA/UK users may also complain to their local supervisory authority.

To exercise any of these, email aniket.kushwaha@hairoriginals.com. We respond within the period required by applicable law.

Children

The app is not intended for anyone under 16, and we do not knowingly collect data — least of all facial images — from anyone below that age. If you believe someone underage has used the app, contact us and we will delete the data.

Grievance officer (India)

Under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023, Indian users may contact our Grievance Officer, Mayank Sharma, at feedback@hairoriginals.com or +91 9267993875.
